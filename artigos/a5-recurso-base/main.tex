\begin{abstract}
Public benchmarks for product-title classification are scarce, rarely in
Portuguese, and almost never ship with an audit of their own label
noise. We release \todo{condicional à decisão de licenciamento} a corpus
of $\approx$250{,}000 short retail product descriptions from Brazilian
fiscal receipts (4--50 characters, uppercase, aggressive abbreviation),
labeled over a closed catalog of 621 product-type categories with severe
long-tail imbalance --- together with the three artifacts that make it a
\emph{measurement-grade} resource rather than a raw dump: (i) a
\textbf{label-noise audit}: a census of conflicting labels (identical
normalized text, divergent gold), a multi-gold re-scoring protocol, and
the resulting measurement ceiling ($\approx$99.3\%), shown to shift
oracle accuracies by at most 0.7 points without reordering any
comparison; (ii) \textbf{paired evaluation samples} (random $n=1{,}000$;
stratified 3-per-class $n=1{,}863$) with zero-shot annotations from six
LLM oracles under a recorded (model, provider, contract, batch,
temperature, date) provenance tuple --- enabling instrument-level
replication studies; and (iii) an open \textbf{active-learning library}
(hexagonal architecture; prototype, linear and transformer classifiers;
uncertainty, density and stratified selection policies; LLM oracle
adapters with strict output validation, annotation caching and cost
metering) plus a visual pipeline builder, with which every published
number is reproducible from versioned experiment states. Baselines span
a per-class prototype model (89.6\% accuracy at full supervision),
fine-tuned BERTimbau, and the LLM-oracle plateau (77--83\% zero-shot).
The resource targets research on label-efficient learning, LLM
annotation, and evaluation methodology in lower-resourced languages.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} language resources; Portuguese; short text;
product classification; label noise; active learning; reproducibility.

\section{Motivation}
\label{sec:motivation}

Three research communities need this resource and currently improvise.
Work on \emph{LLM annotation} needs closed-taxonomy tasks at realistic
granularity with audited gold --- published product studies stop at
tens to $\approx$370 categories \citep{Roumeliotis2025,Gholamian2024},
and public product corpora with real noise exist mainly in English or
multilingual marketplace settings \citep{Raczkowska2024AlleNoise,
Karl2023}. Work on \emph{label-efficient learning} (active learning,
semi-supervision) needs pools large enough for full-trajectory
experiments with a reserved population. Work on \emph{Portuguese} NLP
for commerce exists at scale only behind institutional walls
\citep{Machado2026RetailPt}. This resource serves the three at once,
and adds what none of the cited resources provides: an audit of its own
gold standard and per-annotation LLM provenance.

\section{Corpus}
\label{sec:corpus}

$\approx$250k product descriptions as typed into Brazilian point-of-sale
systems and printed on fiscal receipts: uppercase, 4--50 characters,
domain abbreviations (\texttt{CERV} for beer, \texttt{LT} for can),
brand and packaging tokens interleaved with type information. Labels:
621 product-type categories (e.g., \emph{cerveja}, \emph{enxaguante
bucal}), assigned by a human back-office process over years of
operation \citep{Daru2022,Daru2024Dissertacao}. After per-class
filtering ($\ge 2$ instances) and text-normalized deduplication the
experimental view has 231{,}490 unique texts across 649 raw classes.
The distribution is heavily long-tailed --- the property that makes the
benchmark hard: macro metrics are governed by hundreds of small classes.

\section{The label-noise audit}
\label{sec:audit}

Real gold standards err; a resource that hides it exports silent bias.
We ship: (i) the \textbf{conflict census} --- groups of identical
normalized texts carrying divergent labels, with counts and examples
\todo{inserir estatísticas exatas do censo (nº de grupos, \% de
instâncias afetadas) a partir do artefato da auditoria};
(ii) a \textbf{multi-gold protocol} --- re-scoring any system accepting
any of the conflicting labels as correct; (iii) the \textbf{sensitivity
result}: across six LLM oracles, excluding conflicts or applying
multi-gold shifts accuracy by at most $+0.7$ points and never reorders
systems --- so the corpus supports comparative research despite its
measured $\approx$0.7\% gold-noise floor, and the measurement ceiling
($\approx$99.3\%) should accompany any absolute claim.

\section{Paired LLM annotation samples}
\label{sec:samples}

Two evaluation samples, annotated by six oracles (three cost regimes:
commercial APIs, MaaS platforms, free tier) at temperature 0 with
per-annotation provenance: \textbf{S-rand} ($n=1{,}000$, production
distribution) and \textbf{S-strat} (3 per class, $n=1{,}863$, tail
support). Every annotation records model, serving provider, output
contract (enum / json-prompt / free), batch size, token counts and
date --- the metadata that companion work shows can move results more
than model choice. These samples support replication of
instrument-effect studies (output-contract artifact; serving-provider
divergence) without any API spending.

\section{Library and pipeline builder}
\label{sec:library}

The \texttt{activelearning} library (Python, hexagonal architecture ---
domain core isolated from adapters) provides: classifiers (per-class
prototype PVBin, whose full-supervision baseline is 89.6\% accuracy /
0.70 macro-F1; logistic SGD; BERTimbau fine-tuning
\citep{Souza2020BERTimbau}); selection policies (entropy, margin, least
confidence, random, density-representative DRI-SL, prediction-guided and
prediction-stratified variants); oracle adapters (OpenAI-compatible
providers, structured-output and prompted-JSON contracts, strict label
validation, persistent annotation cache, cost metering); loop
orchestration with stagnation-based stopping and state-file resumption;
and a web pipeline builder (FlowBuilder) for no-code experiment
assembly. Every experiment in the companion papers resolves to a
versioned state under \texttt{experiments/}, and a Docker image plus a
Colab notebook reproduce the GPU-dependent baselines.

\section{Baseline summary}
\label{sec:baselines}

Full supervision: PVBin 89.6\% / 0.70 macro-F1; BERTimbau
\todo{números do braço D do E3'}. Zero-shot LLM plateau: 77--83\%
accuracy, macro-F1 up to 0.80 (exceeding the light supervised baseline
on the tail). Label-efficient reference points: entropy-based selection
reaches 95\% of its ceiling with 16--38\% of pool labels depending on
the classifier. These anchors let new methods report gains against
published, artifact-backed numbers.
