\begin{abstract}
Surveys of practitioners report that the barriers to adopting active
learning (AL) with large language models are operational --- deployment
complexity, uncertain cost reduction, inadequate tooling --- rather than
accuracy. FALCO answers with a framework whose every decision is an
audited, pre-registered instrument: a label-free cold start (DRI-SL);
uncertainty-driven selection with a cheap classifier in the loop;
a \emph{progressive oracle} policy where a budget-quality menu of LLM
annotators is measured first (accuracy with confidence intervals, cost
per thousand labels with cache and batch accounting) and a pre-registered
gate decides which oracle serves each phase; stagnation-based stopping;
and a release-plus-drift policy that reuses the cheap oracle as a
continuous audit instrument. We report a complete end-to-end execution at
\emph{zero oracle cost} on a 621-class Portuguese retail task: with a
free-tier LLM oracle ($\approx$78--80\% accuracy), the loop stopped
itself by validation stagnation at 32--40\% of a 15{,}000-label budget.
Validation deliberately keeps the strong classifier (BERTimbau) out of
the loop, training it once per arm on collected sets: oracle noise costs
4.4 accuracy points against gold labels on identical instances; actively
selected sets cover 620 of 649 classes against 493 for random sets of
equal size; and an entropy-selected 15k set reaches 83.1\% population
accuracy. \todo{fechar com braço D: veredito da hipótese
$F1(A) \ge 0{,}95 \cdot F1(D)$}
Noise-robustness experiments bound the damage of oracle error (87\% of
final macro-F1 retained at 10\% uniform noise, 74\% at 20\%), and the
framework's measurement layer contributed two instrument-level findings
(output-contract artifact; serving-provider divergence) reported in
companion work. Code, prompts, per-annotation artifacts and a
reproducible runner are released.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Two decades of active learning research minimized \emph{label counts}
under an assumed-correct oracle \citep{Settles2012,Olsson2009}; cost-aware
work showed counts are not costs \citep{SettlesCravenFriedland2008}; and
the LLM-as-annotator turn \citep{Gilardi2023,Zhang2025,Rouzegar2024,
Kholodna2024} changed the currency twice: cost became a function of
tokens (and caching, and batching), and oracle quality became a
\emph{purchasable parameter} on a menu spanning an order of magnitude in
price. Yet community surveys \citep{Romberg2025Reassessing} find adoption
blocked by the same barriers as fifteen years ago: deployment complexity,
uncertain real savings, inadequate tooling.

FALCO (\emph{Framework de Aprendizado Ativo com LLM para texto CurtO})
is our answer: an AL framework for long-tailed short-text classification
whose distinguishing property is not a new selection strategy but an
\emph{operational discipline} --- every component decision (which oracle,
which phase, when to stop, when to release, when to retrain) is derived
from a measured, pre-registered, artifact-backed criterion. This paper
presents the framework and its complete execution and validation on a
hard industrial task: 621 imbalanced product categories, Portuguese
fiscal-receipt descriptions of 4--50 characters.

\section{The framework}
\label{sec:framework}

FALCO composes four components, each validated separately in companion
work and integrated here. \todo{figura da arquitetura (fases + gate +

\paragraph{Phase 1 --- label-free cold start.} The initial set is built by
DRI-SL, a deterministic heuristic (semantic-density clustering plus
lexical-variety filling) that outperforms even a supervised evolutionary
optimizer of $L_0$ composition in the 100--5{,}000 range. Phase 1 spends
at most 1\% of the label budget.

\paragraph{Phase 2 --- uncertainty selection with a cheap learner.} The
loop retrains a light classifier per batch (per-class prototype PVBin or
logistic SGD \citep{Daru2024Dissertacao}); entropy selection dominates at
scale (saturating at 95\% of its ceiling with 16--38\% of the pool,
versus 80\%+ for random). The strong classifier stays \emph{out} of the
loop by design --- it is trained once, at the end, on the collected set;
this is both the cost model and, as Section~\ref{sec:validation} shows,
the correct validation design.

\paragraph{Progressive oracle with a pre-registered gate.} Candidate LLM
oracles are measured first on paired samples (Wilson intervals; McNemar
comparisons; cost per thousand labels with provider-metered tokens,
cache and batch amortization). A gate fixed \emph{before} measurement
maps results to architecture: the Initial oracle is the best
accuracy/cost ratio; an Advanced oracle exists only if statistically
superior to the Initial ($\alpha=0.05$); if no candidate clears an 85\%
accuracy bar, noisy-oracle training becomes the central scenario and
robustness (below) is mandatory. On our task the gate selected
deepseek-v4-flash (78--82\% at US\$0.035/1k) as Initial and
deepseek-v4-pro as Advanced ($p<0.001$), with a zero-cost free-tier
oracle (nemotron-3-ultra, statistically tied with the Initial,
$p=0.76$) as the no-budget alternative.

\paragraph{Stopping, release, drift.} The loop stops on the first of:
validation-macro-F1 stagnation ($<10^{-3}$ gain over 5 iterations),
budget exhaustion, or pool exhaustion. Release requires stagnation
\emph{plus} the validation Wilson interval clearing a floor; the
untouched test set is consumed exactly once. In production, three
monitoring layers (input drift at zero label cost; confidence drift;
weekly oracle spot-audit at $\approx$US\$0.01 per 200 items) trigger
incremental re-runs of the loop over recent data.

\section{Zero-cost end-to-end execution}
\label{sec:execution}

The full loop ran twice (PVBin and SGD in the loop) with the free oracle
and a 15{,}000-label budget over a 50{,}000-instance pool. Both runs
\textbf{stopped themselves} by validation stagnation --- at 6{,}009
labels (PVBin) and 4{,}742 (SGD), i.e., 32--40\% of budget --- with
validation and test curves flat at the stop point, and 241 invalid oracle
responses out of 6{,}250 oracle calls in the first run (3.9\%) handled
by the framework's strict validation layer.
Monetary oracle cost: zero; the binding constraint was provider
throughput, which the framework's batch calibration (paired McNemar
across batch sizes) and caching layer are designed to manage.

\section{Validation with the strong classifier out of the loop}
\label{sec:validation}

BERTimbau \citep{Souza2020BERTimbau} is fine-tuned once per arm on
already-collected sets and evaluated on a reserved population
($\approx$177k instances) never touched by selection, with a fixed
stratified evaluation sample and Wilson intervals. Arms: (A) the 8{,}937
items annotated by the real pipeline, with the \emph{oracle's} labels;
(B) the same items with gold labels; (C) equal-size random sets with
gold; (D) the full 50k pool with gold --- the ruler; (E) a 15k
entropy-selected prefix with gold.

Findings so far: oracle noise costs \textbf{4.4 accuracy points}
(A: 65.9\% [65.2, 66.5] vs.\ B: 70.2\% [69.6, 70.9]) --- the price of
71.6\% oracle-gold agreement on \emph{actively selected} (hence harder)
instances; random selection wins raw accuracy at equal size (C: 73.2\%)
while active selection wins class coverage (620 vs.\ 493 classes) and
macro-F1 (A: 0.242, B: 0.209, C: 0.189); and 15k entropy-selected gold
labels reach \textbf{83.1\%} population accuracy (E), showing the strong
classifier converts well-chosen additional labels efficiently.

\paragraph{Robustness to oracle noise.} Controlled uniform-noise
experiments with the light classifier bound the damage: 87\% of final
macro-F1 retained at $\varepsilon=0.1$, 74\% at $\varepsilon=0.2$, 54\%
at $\varepsilon=0.4$. Since real oracle error is \emph{structured}
(concentrated in sibling categories), and structured noise of equal rate
is more benign than uniform \citep{Frenay2014,Song2023NoisyLabels} ---
with the caveat that real-noise benchmarks warn against overclaiming
\citep{Merdjanovska2024NoiseBench,Raczkowska2024AlleNoise} --- the
uniform design is a deliberately pessimistic test.

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

LLM-in-the-loop AL spans annotator \citep{Zhang2025}, selector
\citep{Bayer2024}, confidence routing \citep{Rouzegar2024}, batch
annotation \citep{Kholodna2024}, candidate-label distillation
\citep{Xia2025CanDist}, LLM mixtures \citep{Qi2026MixtureLLMs} and
dual-expert taxonomy contraction \citep{Cheng2024DualExpert}; cold-start
seeding is addressed by DEUCE \citep{Guo2025Deuce} among others. Applied
Portuguese retail classification at scale exists without AL or oracle
automation \citep{Machado2026RetailPt}. FALCO's contribution is not any
single mechanism but the integration under measurement discipline ---
the combination of informed cold start + progressive measured oracle +
instrumented cost + pre-registered decisions, which, to our knowledge,
no prior framework offers end to end.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

FALCO shows that the practical barriers to LLM-assisted active learning
--- cost uncertainty, deployment opacity, unverifiable claims --- are
addressable by treating measurement as part of the framework: oracles
are purchased off a measured menu through a pre-registered gate; the
loop stops itself; the strong model trains once; release requires a
reserved-set criterion; and production monitoring reuses the cheap
oracle as an audit instrument. A complete execution at zero oracle cost,
stopping at a third of budget with the strong-classifier validation
quantifying every loss source (noise vs.\ selection vs.\ budget),
demonstrates the discipline is affordable. \todo{frase final com o
