\begin{abstract}

Short-text classification in Portuguese faces abbreviated and noisy
vocabulary, as well as hundreds of imbalanced categories, as in product
descriptions from electronic invoices. Supervised methods depend on large
volumes of labels, and labeling is the dominant cost of these systems. A way
to reduce that cost without relevant loss of final performance is still
lacking. This thesis proposes and evaluates FALCO (Active Learning Framework
with LLMs for Short Text), which reduces labeling cost in three stages. The
first is informed composition of the initial set without using labels,
through the DRI-SL heuristic; the second is uncertainty-based active
selection. The third is partial replacement of the human annotator by oracles
based on large language models (LLMs), organized in phases of increasing
cost. The evaluation uses an audited public dataset of 250,365 descriptions
across 621 categories, of which 250,221 in the corrected version used in the
experiments. The protocol is pre-registered, with immutable partitions,
paired tests (McNemar, Wilcoxon), and a traceable artifact for every reported
number. Final validation runs the framework end to end, with a free LLM
oracle and the BERTimbau classifier, across three seeds, with evaluation on
the reserved population of 177,490 instances. DRI-SL outperforms the best
individual found by a supervised genetic algorithm without using any label,
and the advantage holds at every initial-set size from 100 to 5,000.
Zero-shot LLM oracles reach 77--83\% accuracy at US\$~0.035--0.92 per
thousand labels. Their Macro F1 exceeds that of a light supervised classifier
trained on the full base, with the caveat that per-class support differs
between the two measurements. Without output restriction to the class space,
$6.8\%$ of responses are counted as spurious errors, and the same model
diverges from itself across serving providers ($p<0.001$). Uncertainty-based
selection outperforms random selection ($p=0.0078$) and keeps the advantage
under oracle noise up to $\varepsilon=0.4$. Since no oracle reaches the
pre-registered $85\%$ accuracy criterion, learning with noisy labels becomes
the central scenario. The central hypothesis is to obtain at least $95\%$ of
the accuracy of full supervision with at most 34,724 labels. The verdict is
twofold: the criterion is attainable, and the crossing occurs at 20,000
labels ($8.6\%$ of the population) in all three seeds, in a \textit{post hoc}
gold-label sweep. The executed configuration, however, stops by stagnation at
11,936 labels ($5.2\%$). The paired decomposition attributes the loss to the
stopping criterion, not to oracle noise or selection. Two limitations bound
the scope: the results come from a single dataset, from the author's own
study domain, and the cost analysis rests on ratios between oracles rather
than absolute prices. The thesis also delivers the LCE (Learning Curve
Efficiency) metric, the open-source \texttt{activelearning} library, and the
FlowBuilder tool, which make the protocol reusable.

\end{abstract}
